Imagine an entire city too afraid to sleep — every window locked, and still, no one felt safe.

In the summer of 1985, Los Angeles lived in fear of a man the newspapers called the Night Stalker.

He slipped into homes at night, choosing victims at random. Thirteen people were killed — strangers to him, strangers to each other.

For more than a year, the police had almost nothing: different guns, different neighborhoods, and no motive anyone could see.

Then one fingerprint changed everything — lifted from the mirror of a stolen car, it matched a 25-year-old named Richard Ramirez.

His photograph ran in every newspaper. On August 31st, 1985, ordinary people in East Los Angeles spotted him on the street — and caught him themselves.

He was convicted of thirteen murders in 1989, and died on death row in 2013, never executed.

A city that could not sleep finally did — because its people stopped waiting for someone else to catch him. Would you have recognized him? Follow — tomorrow, same time, another case with no answer.
