Three men walked into a lighthouse — and never walked out.

On a bare Atlantic rock stands the Flannan Isles lighthouse. Three keepers tended its light: James Ducat, Thomas Marshall, Donald McArthur.

On December 13th, the log stops. The readings for the 15th were left on a slate, never logged. The lamp was ready. The work was done. Then — nothing.

Around midnight, the steamer Archtor sailed past — and the light was dark.

Eleven days later, the ship Hesperus reached the rock. The station was empty. No keepers. No struggle. No trace.

The inquiry blamed a giant wave at the west landing. A supply box was found smashed — thirty-three metres above the sea.

But no one saw it happen. A wave that high, and three men gone without a trace? A hundred years on, we still don't know.

Three keepers. One dark lighthouse. Would you stay on that rock alone? And follow — tomorrow, same time, another case that was never solved.
