You've seen the photo — a long neck rising from a dark Scottish lake. For ninety years, people have sworn a monster lives in Loch Ness, a lake twenty-three miles long and seven hundred feet deep. In 1933, a couple said a huge creature crossed the road in front of their car, and the newspapers went wild. A year later, the most famous photo of the monster appeared — the Surgeon's Photograph. For sixty years, it was the best evidence anyone had. Then, in 1994, the truth came out. It was a toy submarine with a plastic head, built as a prank. But the sightings never stopped — over a thousand of them. In 2018, scientists tested the DNA of the entire lake. No reptile. No monster. Just a lot of eel DNA. So is Nessie a giant eel — or just a story we can't let go of?
