A killer mailed his crimes to the newspapers — and signed every letter with a target. Fifty years later, you still don't know his name.

Between December 1968 and October 1969, the man the press called the Zodiac killed five people around San Francisco Bay. Two more survived.

He sent more than twenty letters to the Chronicle, the Examiner, and the Vallejo Times-Herald — taunting police, threatening more murders, and hiding his identity inside secret codes.

One cipher, four hundred and eight symbols, was cracked in a week by a schoolteacher couple. But the second, three hundred and forty symbols, resisted every codebreaker on Earth for fifty-one years. When it was finally solved in 2020, it named no one.

Police named only one suspect, Arthur Leigh Allen, a former teacher from Vallejo. He died in 1992, never charged. DNA didn't match.

So who was the Zodiac — a killer who vanished, or a man who was never really there?
