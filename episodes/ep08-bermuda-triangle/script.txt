Draw a triangle between Miami, Bermuda, and Puerto Rico — and you enter the most dangerous water on Earth. Or so you've been told.

On December 5th, 1945, five Navy bombers, Flight 19, vanished over that ocean. Fourteen airmen, gone.

A rescue plane sent to find them disappeared too. Twenty-seven men, six planes, no wreckage.

It wasn't the first. In 1918, the USS Cyclops, a Navy ship with more than three hundred aboard, sailed into the same waters and never came out.

In 1963, the Marine Sulphur Queen, thirty-nine crew, vanished the same way.

But here's the part nobody puts on the poster: the U.S. Coast Guard says the Triangle is no more dangerous than any other stretch of ocean.

Lloyd's of London charges no extra insurance to cross it.

So did the ocean swallow them — or did we invent a monster to explain ordinary tragedy?
