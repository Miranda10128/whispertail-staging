Imagine an entire town — 115 people — vanishing overnight. Not a battle. Not a plague. Just... gone.

In 1587, 115 English colonists landed on Roanoke Island, off the coast of North Carolina. Among them was a newborn baby, Virginia Dare — the first English child born in America.

Their governor, John White, sailed back to England for supplies. But war with Spain — the Spanish Armada — trapped him there for three years.

When he finally returned in 1590, the settlement was empty. The houses had been taken apart. No bodies. No graves. No sign of a fight. Just one word, carved into a wooden post: CROATOAN.

It was the name of a nearby island — and of a friendly tribe. Did the colonists join them? Did they move inland? Or did something else take them?

Four hundred years later, we still don't know. So tell me — where do you think 115 people went? And follow — tomorrow, same time, another case that was never solved.
