A man jumped out of a passenger plane with two hundred thousand dollars strapped to his body — and was never seen again. No body. No parachute. Not a single dollar, for nine years.

It was the night before Thanksgiving, 1971. A calm man in a dark suit boarded Flight 305 in Portland, ordered a bourbon, and handed the flight attendant a note: he had a bomb. His price? Two hundred thousand dollars, and four parachutes.

In Seattle, he got every cent, released the passengers, and ordered the plane back into the sky. Somewhere over the forests of southwest Washington, he opened the rear stairs of the Boeing 727 and stepped into the dark.

The FBI searched for eighteen days. Nothing. Then in 1980, a little boy digging on a riverbank found bundles of rotting twenty-dollar bills — the serial numbers matched.

Fifty-four years later, no one knows who he was, or whether he survived the jump. So tell me — did D.B. Cooper live, or did the forest keep him?
