A man was found dead on a beach in Australia. No wallet. No ID. Every label cut out of his clothes. And in a secret pocket, a tiny scrap of paper with two words: Tamám Shud. It means — it is finished.

It was December 1st, 1948. A well-dressed man lay against the seawall at Somerton Beach, Adelaide. He looked like he'd simply fallen asleep. But he was dead. And no one knew who he was, or how he died.

Months later, police found the paper had been torn from the last page of a book of Persian poetry — the Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam. A man came forward with that very book. Inside the back cover, written in pencil, was a phone number — and five lines of letters. A code.

Navy codebreakers tried. Intelligence agencies tried. No one ever cracked it.

Seventy-eight years later, the code is still unbroken. So tell me — was the Somerton Man a spy, or just a man who wanted to disappear?
